# Epoch, anchor and precision

Each display callback services the existing assembly once, processes Solar input and publishes Solar time, then copies the assembly focus observation at that Solar display epoch. Final camera placement and camera-relative submission follow. Rendering refreshes the same existing Florida mapping without intervening simulation or Solar publication.

Physical observation time and Solar browsing time remain separate, explicit fields. This preserves the banked bounded Florida episode and held endpoint; it does not add interpolation, extrapolation, smoothing, warp, or moving Florida departure. Paused Solar time still accepts new coherent copied physical observations. A display-epoch mismatch retires the camera binding instead of displaying a stale target.

The visible anchor is canonical material origin **O**, mapped through the existing Florida/Solar presentation. It is not changing center of mass. The test checks that the material-O rendered mesh and camera target use exactly the same root position. The moving qualified free-flight episode actually consumes resources and changes COM while the focus remains attached to O.

Target and camera world positions remain FP64 `UniversePosition`/`Double3`. Subtraction precedes the existing camera-relative encoded FP32 transport. Copied-observation translation proofs cover 100 m, 1 km and 100 km; precision roots reach `(4e12,-3e12,7e12)` m. These are bounded camera fixtures, not new flight trajectories. Numerical centering is required within 2 mm and encoded relative reconstruction within 1 micrometre. Visual acceptance remains Project Control's gate.

The environmental index remains Earth while SRV is viewed. Existing terrain exclusion, physical dataset and planetary representation remain authoritative. Vessel near clip is bounded by orbit distance and environmental clip, enabling close inspection at large world positions. Detached near clip is refreshed from the existing environmental altitude without changing viewport aspect.
